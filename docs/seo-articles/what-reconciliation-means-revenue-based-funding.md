# What Reconciliation Means in Revenue-Based Funding

Reconciliation is one of the most important concepts in revenue-based funding.

In a receivables-purchase structure, remittance should make sense in relation to actual business revenue. If a business's revenue changes materially, reconciliation is the process for reviewing whether the remittance amount should be adjusted according to the agreement.

Business owners should understand this before signing any merchant cash advance or revenue-based funding agreement.

## What Reconciliation Means

Reconciliation is a review process. It compares the amount being remitted to the business's actual revenue or receivables activity during the relevant period.

The goal is not to create a vague promise. The goal is to have a clear written process that explains:

- When reconciliation can be requested.
- What documents or records are needed.
- How revenue is measured.
- How remittance may be adjusted.
- How quickly the request will be reviewed.
- What happens while the request is pending.

If reconciliation is important to the structure, it should appear in the agreement, not only in a sales conversation.

## Revenue Percentage Is the Cleanest Starting Point

The cleanest form of revenue-based remittance is often a percentage of actual revenue or receivables. If sales rise, remittance may rise. If sales fall, remittance may fall.

That can better match the idea of a receivables purchase, but it also requires accurate reporting and cooperation. The business owner needs to understand what records must be shared and how often.

FORZA CAPITAL PARTNERS LLC plans to prioritize revenue-percentage structures where appropriate.

## Weekly ACH With Reconciliation

Some businesses and funders use weekly ACH because it is operationally simpler. A weekly amount can be easier to administer, but it should still have a real reconciliation process if the agreement is meant to track receivables.

A good weekly ACH structure should answer:

- What revenue assumptions were used?
- When can the merchant request review?
- What happens if actual sales are lower than expected?
- What happens if actual sales are higher than expected?
- How are adjustments documented?

Without those details, a weekly payment can become stressful when sales change.

## Daily Fixed ACH Can Be Higher Stress

Daily fixed ACH is common in some MCA structures, but it can create pressure if sales drop. A business owner should understand why daily remittance is being used and how reconciliation works.

FORZA's launch plan treats daily fixed ACH as a higher-risk structure, not the default. It should be reserved for files where the risk profile supports it and counsel-approved reconciliation terms are clear.

## What Records May Be Needed

A reconciliation request may require:

- Recent bank statements.
- Merchant processing statements.
- Sales reports.
- Deposit records.
- Current receivables data.
- Explanation of unusual revenue changes.

The exact requirements should be written into the agreement. Business owners should not wait until revenue drops to learn what records are needed.

## What Reconciliation Does Not Mean

Reconciliation does not mean automatic forgiveness. It does not mean the business can stop cooperating. It does not mean every request will produce a lower remittance amount.

It means the agreement has a process for comparing remittance with actual revenue and making any contractually supported adjustment.

That process protects both sides when it is clear.

## Questions to Ask Before Signing

Before signing, ask:

- Is remittance based on a percentage of revenue or a fixed amount?
- If fixed, what revenue assumption was used?
- Can I request reconciliation?
- How do I request it?
- What documents are required?
- How long does review take?
- Can remittance change up or down?
- Who confirms the adjustment?

If the answers are unclear, the agreement needs more review.
